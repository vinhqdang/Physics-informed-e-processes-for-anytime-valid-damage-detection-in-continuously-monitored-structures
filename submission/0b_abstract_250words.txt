Damage thresholds are usually set as if one test were run, so the chance of a false alarm over a monitoring life can approach one. This paper develops PHASE (Physics-informed Hedged Anytime-valid Sequential Evidence), a rule with a stated false-alarm budget. PHASE predicts temperature and load effects on natural frequencies, scores the remainder only in directions that stiffness loss can produce (the damage cone), and accumulates it in a betting e-process. The budget is a conditional theorem; its commissioning-based calibration is only empirical, and the provable variant has little power. In 4,200 simulated records, PHASE raised no false alarm in 300 undamaged records at one year or in 300 at three (upper 95% limit 1.2%), detected a 5% loss at storey 3 in all 150 records (median 59 days) and a 2% loss in 34%, and stayed silent under a benign stiffening. Commissioning of 30-60 days destroyed false-alarm control. On the Z24 bridge a reduced version stayed silent when stiffness was recovered; on KW51, with real covariates, it raised alarms before the inferred retrofit period (relative to an assumed healthy reference) in most hourly configurations. Only simulation validates the full framework. The bound that each structure has probability at most alpha of ever raising a first false alarm holds only if the expected score of the undamaged structure stays below the design constant plus the calibrated allowance, which requires commissioning that represents the whole operating environment; coverage alone is not sufficient. Missed detections are not controlled.
